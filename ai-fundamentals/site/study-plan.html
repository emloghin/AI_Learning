Study Plan: Your Complete AI Learning Path

A complete, week-by-week plan that combines the chapters of this site with the best free and low-cost AI courses from Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Anthropic and the University of Helsinki — plus a daily practice habit, because applying AI to your own work is what turns learning into results.

1. How the plan works

Every path mixes three ingredients:

Ingredient What it gives you Where it comes from
Understand How AI models work, so you can judge what they can and cannot do Chapters 1–9 of this site, Elements of AI
Use Hands-on skill with real AI tools and good prompting habits Google AI Essentials, OpenAI Academy, Claude Academy, Chapter 6
Apply Visible results on your own daily work Chapter 10: AI at Work and the weekly practice tasks below

📘 In plain words: Learn a little, use it the same week on a real task, and write down what happened. Repeat. That loop beats binge-watching a course.

2. Choose your path

If you… Take Time
Use AI rarely and want a solid, practical foundation Path A: AI-literate professional 4 weeks · ~3 h/week
Already use ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot and want to get much better at it Path B: AI power user 6 weeks · ~4 h/week
Want to build AI solutions or earn a Microsoft certification Path C: AI builder 8 weeks · ~5 h/week

The paths stack: B assumes A's basics, and C assumes B's. If you already know a topic, skip that week.

🎯 Try it: Paste the "learning plan" prompt from Chapter 10, §7 into your AI assistant to adapt the path you picked to your role and calendar.

3. Path A — AI-literate professional (4 weeks)

Goal: understand what AI is, use a chat assistant confidently and safely, and apply it to two or three of your own tasks.

Week Learn Practise on your own work Deliverable
1 Home "big picture" + Chapter 1: Foundations · Elements of AI, chapters 1–2 List the tasks in your week that take over 15 minutes (Chapter 10, §2) Your task inventory
2 Google AI Essentials or OpenAI Academy "Apply AI at Work" (first modules) · Chapter 6, §2: Prompting Do one real task per day with an approved AI assistant 3 tasks where AI clearly helped
3 Claude Academy: AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations · Chapter 9, §5–6: Privacy & Responsible AI Rewrite your best prompts; start a prompt library 3 saved prompts
4 Claude Academy: AI Capabilities and Limitations · Chapter 10: AI at Work Log time and quality for every AI-assisted task One-page "how I use AI for X"

✅ Key takeaway: After Path A you can explain AI in plain words, prompt well, know the safety rules, and point to real tasks you do better.

4. Path B — AI power user (6 weeks)

Goal: understand why models behave as they do, master prompting and context, and build repeatable AI workflows for your role.

Week Learn Practise on your own work Deliverable
1 Chapter 2: Neural Networks · 3Blue1Brown neural network videos Explain to a colleague, in 2 minutes, what "training" means Your own one-paragraph explanation
2 Chapter 3: Transformers & LLMs · Chapter 8: Tokens & Cost Paste text into a tokenizer; compare long vs short prompts A note on context limits in your tools
3 Anthropic Prompt Engineering Interactive Tutorial, chapters 1–5 Apply each technique to one of your saved prompts Improved prompt library
4 Anthropic tutorial, chapters 6–9 · Chapter 6: Using Models §2–3 and Chapter 7: Connecting Models §1–4 Use examples and step-by-step reasoning on a complex task A multi-step workflow (e.g. notes → summary → email)
5 OpenAI Academy "Apply AI at Work" (workflows and agents) · Chapter 5: Model Landscape Try the same task in two different assistants and compare A short comparison table
6 Chapter 9: Best Practices · Chapter 10, §5–6 Measure a month of AI use; share your best workflow 5-minute team demo

5. Path C — AI builder (8 weeks)

Goal: build simple AI-powered solutions, understand training and evaluation, and prepare for the Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals certification (exam AI-901).

Before you start: Microsoft expects AI-901 candidates to know basic Python syntax and to be familiar with Azure resources. If you are new to either, add a free Python basics course and the Azure fundamentals material on Microsoft Learn before week 2.

Week Learn Build / practise Deliverable
1 Chapter 4: Training & Tuning · Google ML Crash Course (first modules) Train a tiny network in TensorFlow Playground Screenshot + what you changed and why
2 Microsoft Learn: Introduction to AI in Azure (AI-901 course), concepts part Explore Microsoft Foundry's model catalogue Notes mapped to the AI-901 skills outline
3 AI-901 course, Foundry part · Chapter 6, §3 and Chapter 7, §3 (structured outputs, tool calling) Call a model from code or a playground with JSON output Working sample
4 Chapter 7, §1–2: Embeddings & RAG · Microsoft Generative AI for Beginners, RAG lessons Build the mini Q&A bot from Chapter 7, §7 on public or synthetic documents Demo bot
5 Claude Academy: Building with the Claude API or OpenAI Academy "Build with AI" Add tool calling to your bot Bot with one tool
6 Claude Academy: Introduction to Model Context Protocol · Chapter 7, §5: MCP Connect an assistant to a tool via MCP MCP experiment notes
7 Chapter 9, §3–4: Evaluation & Security · OWASP Top 10 for LLM Apps Write 10 test cases for your bot; try a prompt-injection attack on it Eval results + fixes
8 AI-901 practice assessment (free; linked from the certification page, runs on Microsoft AI Skills Navigator) · review weak areas Take the practice assessment until you pass comfortably Exam booked or passed

⚠️ Common misconception: "A certificate proves I can build AI solutions." AI-901 is a fundamentals exam. The bot, the evaluation and the security test from weeks 4–7 are what show you can build.

Optional builder workshop: spec to production

After you can run and test a small application, use Chapter 13 to practise AI-assisted software delivery. This is separate from the AI-901 exam path; your product does not need to contain an AI model.

Session Practise Deliverable
0 Equip your agent with instructions, one skill, one read-only MCP server and a reviewer agent; apply the control checklist A prepared repository and a ticked control checklist
1 Set up Spec Kit, write one bounded feature and resolve ambiguities Reviewed spec with observable acceptance criteria
2 Review the plan, tasks and analysis; implement with one agent Small working change with tests you have actually run
3 Try Squad on independent remaining tasks, then review the combined result A recorded task handoff and evidence of what each role changed
4 Adopt Spec Kit in an existing project: baseline, evidence-based constitution and one bounded change The adoption commit, a reviewed constitution and one merged change
5 Follow Shipping and Reuse, including a release rehearsal PR checklist, rollback plan and a reusable project setup checklist

Keep the command reference open while you practise.

Start with synthetic data and a learning repository. The tutorial is not permission to deploy an unreviewed application.

7. Stay current: a 30-minute weekly routine

AI tools change every month. A short, fixed routine keeps you up to date without drowning in hype.

Time Activity Suggested sources
10 min News & new tools — what was released this week? Matt Wolfe on YouTube and his Future Tools site and newsletter (AI news and a searchable tool directory)
10 min Primary sources — changelogs of the tools you actually use Provider docs and release notes (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, GitHub Copilot, Microsoft — see Resources)
10 min Try one thing — test one new feature or tool on a real task Your own work, logged as in Chapter 10, §5

⚠️ Common misconception: "If a popular creator shows it, it works for my job." News channels and tool directories are great for discovering tools, but some listings are sponsored or affiliate links and demos show best cases. Check that a tool is approved by your organisation and test it on your own (anonymised) work before relying on it.

8. Track your progress

Tick these off as you go. Your ticks are saved in this browser.

Understand

  • I can explain the difference between AI, machine learning, deep learning and generative AI (Ch. 1)
  • I can explain what parameters, training and inference are (Ch. 1, Ch. 2)
  • I can explain tokens, context windows and why models hallucinate (Ch. 3, Ch. 8)
  • I finished at least one external foundation course (Elements of AI, Google AI Essentials, OpenAI Academy or Anthropic AI Fluency)

Use

  • I use clear task, context, examples and format in my prompts (Ch. 6)
  • I know which AI tools my organisation approves and what data I may not paste (Ch. 9)
  • I completed the Anthropic Prompt Engineering Interactive Tutorial

Apply

  • I have a list of my AI-ready tasks (Ch. 10)
  • I keep a prompt library with at least 5 prompts
  • I logged AI use for 4 weeks and know where it helps and where it does not
  • I shared one workflow with my team

Build (Path C)

  • I built a small RAG bot with tests and a prompt-injection check
  • I passed the AI-901 practice assessment / exam
  • Optional: I wrote one skill and one custom agent, added a read-only MCP server, and know which permission level my agent sessions use (Ch. 11, Ch. 12)
  • Optional: I delivered one bounded feature with Spec Kit, tried a reviewed Squad handoff, and rehearsed the release checklist (Ch. 13)

✅ Key takeaway: Pick one path, combine one course with this site's chapters, and practise on your own work every week. Completed checklists and a log of real results are worth more than a pile of half-finished courses.
