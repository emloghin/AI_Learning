5. Key papers

You do not need to read these to use AI, but each one changed the field. The arXiv abstract pages are free to read.

Resource Type Level Why it's useful (one-line summary) Link
Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani et al., 2017) Paper Advanced Introduced the transformer: attention alone, no recurrence, which became the basis of modern LLMs. arXiv:1706.03762
Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3, Brown et al., 2020) Paper Advanced Showed that a very large language model can do new tasks from just a few examples in the prompt. arXiv:2005.14165
Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (InstructGPT, Ouyang et al., 2022) Paper Advanced Used RLHF to make a model follow instructions; a smaller tuned model was preferred over a much larger untuned one. arXiv:2203.02155
LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models (Hu et al., 2021) Paper Advanced Fine-tune huge models cheaply by training small add-on matrices instead of all the weights. arXiv:2106.09685
Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Chinchilla, Hoffmann et al., 2022) Paper Advanced Found that for a fixed compute budget, model size and training data should grow together; many models were under-trained. arXiv:2203.15556
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO, Rafailov et al., 2023) Paper Advanced Aligns models directly from preference pairs, skipping the separate reward model used in RLHF. arXiv:2305.18290
LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models (Touvron et al., 2023) Paper Advanced Released strong models trained on public data, kick-starting the open-weight model wave. arXiv:2302.13971
